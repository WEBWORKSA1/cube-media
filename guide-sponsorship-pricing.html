How to Price Brand Sponsorships (With Rate Formulas)

By Cube.Media Editorial10 min readUpdated September 2026

Underpricing is the most expensive mistake a creator makes. Overpricing is the second. This guide gives you a defensible formula that brands respect, plus the add-ons most creators forget to charge for.

Contents1. Price on views, not followers2. Apply multipliers3. Price by deliverable4. Usage rights & exclusivity5. Negotiating6. For brands: fair-price check

1. Price on average views, not followers

Brands buy attention. Followers are a vanity metric. Average views are what a sponsor actually gets. Use the median views of your last 10 videos, measured at 30 days, and leave out outliers.

Base formula: Rate = (average views ÷ 1,000) × CPM. For YouTube integrations, a CPM of $15–$35 is a common starting range. A channel averaging 20,000 views at $25 CPM prices at about $500 per integration.

2. Apply multipliers

  • Niche: finance and B2B software audiences are worth 1.3–1.5× because customer lifetime value is high. Entertainment is often 0.8–0.9×.
  • Engagement: above-benchmark engagement justifies about +20%. Check yours with our engagement rate calculator.
  • Audience geography: a US, UK, Canada or Australia-heavy audience supports higher rates.
  • Proof of performance: past campaign conversion data is your strongest lever.

4. Charge for usage rights and exclusivity

If a brand wants to run your video as a paid ad (whitelisting or Spark Ads) or use it on their site, that's a separate licence. Typical add-ons:

  • Paid usage, 30 days: +25–50% of the base fee.
  • Perpetual usage: +100% or more.
  • Category exclusivity (30–90 days): +20–50%, since you're turning down competitors.
  • Rush turnaround (under 7 days): +25%.

5. Negotiating without underselling

  1. Lead with a media kit: average views, audience demographics, engagement and past results.
  2. Quote a package, not a single post. Three integrations at 10% off beats one-off deals for both sides.
  3. Never drop price without dropping scope. If they push back, remove a deliverable or usage rights.
  4. Get it in writing: deliverables, deadlines, revision rounds, payment terms (net-30 at most, 50% upfront for new brands) and disclosure requirements.
Script: "Based on my average of 20k views and a 6% engagement rate, my integration rate is $500. For three integrations over three months I can do $1,350, which includes a 30-day usage licence."

6. For brands: a fair-price check

Ask for screenshots of 30-day average views and audience geography, not just subscriber counts. Compare the quote against the formula above.
